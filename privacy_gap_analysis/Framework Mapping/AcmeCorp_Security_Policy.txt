ACME CORPORATION
INFORMATION SECURITY POLICY
Version 2.1 | Effective Date: January 1, 2025

==============================================================================
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
==============================================================================

Acme Corporation ("Acme" or "the Company") is committed to protecting the
confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets. This
Information Security Policy establishes the framework for managing cybersecurity
risks across the organization.

This policy applies to all Acme employees, contractors, vendors, and third-party
service providers with access to Acme information systems.

==============================================================================
2. GOVERNANCE AND OVERSIGHT
==============================================================================

2.1 Cybersecurity Oversight
The Board of Directors receives quarterly updates on cybersecurity risk from
the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). The CISO reports directly to
the Chief Technology Officer.

2.2 Roles and Responsibilities
The CISO is responsible for:
- Developing and maintaining the information security program
- Conducting annual risk assessments
- Reporting security incidents to executive leadership
- Managing the security operations team

Department heads are responsible for:
- Ensuring their teams complete security awareness training
- Reporting suspected security incidents within 24 hours
- Implementing security controls appropriate to their data classification

2.3 Policy Review and Updates
This policy is reviewed annually by the Security Committee and updated as
needed to address emerging threats and regulatory requirements.

==============================================================================
3. RISK MANAGEMENT
==============================================================================

3.1 Risk Assessment Process
Acme conducts comprehensive risk assessments annually and when significant
changes occur to systems, processes, or the threat landscape.

Risk assessments identify:
- Critical information assets
- Potential threats and vulnerabilities
- Likelihood and impact of security incidents
- Existing controls and gaps
- Recommended risk mitigation strategies

3.2 Risk Appetite and Tolerance
The organization maintains a moderate risk appetite. We accept some risk to
enable business operations but prioritize protection of customer data and
critical systems.

Risk tolerance levels:
- HIGH risk: Requires executive approval and documented mitigation plan
- MEDIUM risk: Requires departmental approval and quarterly review
- LOW risk: May be accepted with standard security controls

3.3 Vulnerability Management
We maintain an inventory of all systems and applications. Security
vulnerabilities are identified through:
- Quarterly vulnerability scans of all internet-facing systems
- Annual penetration testing by external security firms
- Continuous monitoring of security bulletins and threat intelligence

Critical vulnerabilities must be remediated within 30 days. High-severity
vulnerabilities must be remediated within 90 days.

==============================================================================
4. ASSET MANAGEMENT
==============================================================================

4.1 Asset Inventory
The IT department maintains an inventory of:
- Physical servers and workstations (updated quarterly)
- Software applications and licenses (updated annually)
- Network devices and infrastructure (updated as changes occur)

Note: We do not currently maintain a comprehensive inventory of cloud-based
resources or external SaaS applications used by individual departments.

4.2 Asset Classification
Information assets are classified as:
- PUBLIC: Can be freely shared (marketing materials, press releases)
- INTERNAL: For Acme personnel only (policies, procedures, org charts)
- CONFIDENTIAL: Restricted access (customer data, financial records, trade secrets)
- RESTRICTED: Highest security (encryption keys, credentials, merger documents)

4.3 Asset Prioritization
Critical systems are identified based on:
- Business impact if unavailable
- Sensitivity of data processed
- Regulatory compliance requirements

Our payment processing system and customer database are designated as
critical assets requiring enhanced security controls.

==============================================================================
5. IDENTITY AND ACCESS MANAGEMENT
==============================================================================

5.1 User Access Control
Access to systems and data is granted based on job role and business need.
The principle of least privilege is applied to limit access to only what is
necessary to perform assigned duties.

New employee access requests are approved by the hiring manager and provisioned
by IT within 48 hours of start date.

Access is reviewed annually and terminated within 24 hours of employment
separation.

5.2 Authentication Requirements
All users must use strong passwords meeting these requirements:
- Minimum 12 characters
- Mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and special characters
- Changed every 180 days
- Cannot reuse last 5 passwords

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required for:
- All remote access (VPN)
- Administrative accounts
- Access to production systems

Note: MFA is not currently enforced for standard user access to internal
applications or email systems.

5.3 Remote Access
Remote access to Acme systems requires:
- Approved VPN connection
- Company-issued laptop with full-disk encryption
- Up-to-date antivirus software

Personal devices are not permitted to access corporate resources.

5.4 Physical Access Control
Our corporate headquarters has:
- Badge-controlled access to all floors
- Security guards at main entrance during business hours
- Visitor sign-in and escort requirements
- Surveillance cameras in common areas

Server rooms require additional badge authorization and maintain access logs.

Note: Our satellite offices do not currently have badge-controlled access
systems. Physical security relies on building management.

==============================================================================
6. DATA PROTECTION
==============================================================================

6.1 Data Encryption
Data at rest:
- All laptops and mobile devices use full-disk encryption (BitLocker/FileVault)
- Customer payment information is encrypted in our database (AES-256)
- Backup tapes are encrypted before off-site storage

Data in transit:
- All external communications use TLS 1.2 or higher
- VPN connections use AES-256 encryption
- Email containing confidential information should be sent via encrypted email

Note: Internal network traffic between data center servers is not currently
encrypted. We rely on network segmentation for protection.

6.2 Data Retention and Disposal
Data is retained according to legal and business requirements:
- Financial records: 7 years
- Employee records: 7 years after separation
- Customer transaction records: 5 years
- Email: 2 years (automatic deletion)
- System logs: 90 days

Media disposal:
- Hard drives are securely wiped using DoD 5220.22-M standard
- Drives containing highly sensitive data are physically destroyed
- Paper documents are shredded using cross-cut shredders

6.3 Backup and Recovery
Critical systems are backed up daily. Backups are:
- Stored both on-site and at an off-site facility
- Tested quarterly to verify restoration capability
- Encrypted before transmission to off-site location

Recovery Time Objectives (RTO):
- Critical systems: 4 hours
- Important systems: 24 hours
- Standard systems: 72 hours

Recovery Point Objectives (RPO):
- Critical systems: 1 hour (hourly snapshots)
- Important systems: 24 hours (daily backups)
- Standard systems: 24 hours (daily backups)

==============================================================================
7. SECURITY AWARENESS AND TRAINING
==============================================================================

7.1 General Employee Training
All employees must complete security awareness training:
- Upon hire (within first 30 days)
- Annually thereafter

Training covers:
- Password security and phishing recognition
- Data classification and handling procedures
- Incident reporting procedures
- Acceptable use of company resources
- Social engineering awareness

7.2 Role-Based Training
Additional training is required for:
- IT administrators: Annual security administration training
- Developers: Secure coding practices (annual)
- HR personnel: Privacy and data protection (annual)

Note: We do not currently provide specialized cybersecurity training for
executives or board members beyond the general employee training.

7.3 Third-Party Personnel
Contractors and vendors with access to Acme systems must:
- Complete security awareness training before access is granted
- Acknowledge security policies and acceptable use agreement
- Undergo background checks consistent with their level of access

==============================================================================
8. SECURE DEVELOPMENT AND OPERATIONS
==============================================================================

8.1 System Development Lifecycle
Software development follows secure SDLC principles:
- Security requirements are identified during design phase
- Code reviews include security considerations
- Penetration testing is performed before production deployment
- Security patches are applied within 30 days of release

8.2 Configuration Management
All systems are configured according to security baselines:
- Unnecessary services are disabled
- Default passwords are changed before production use
- Security patches are tracked and applied
- Configuration changes require change management approval

Change control process requires:
- Business justification for the change
- Risk assessment of the proposed change
- Approval from change advisory board
- Testing in non-production environment
- Rollback plan in case of issues

8.3 Development and Production Separation
Development, testing, and production environments are logically separated.
Production data may not be used in development or testing environments without
explicit approval and data masking.

Developers do not have administrative access to production systems.
Production deployments follow formal change control procedures.

Note: Currently, some legacy applications do not have separate development
and testing environments due to budget constraints.

8.4 Security Testing
Applications undergo security testing:
- Static code analysis during development
- Dynamic application security testing before production release
- Annual penetration testing by third-party security firm

Web applications are tested for OWASP Top 10 vulnerabilities.

==============================================================================
9. NETWORK SECURITY
==============================================================================

9.1 Network Segmentation
Our network is segmented into:
- External DMZ (web servers, email gateway)
- Internal corporate network (workstations, file servers)
- Production environment (customer-facing applications)
- Management network (IT administration)

Firewalls control traffic between segments based on least-privilege principles.

9.2 Network Monitoring
Network traffic is monitored for:
- Unauthorized access attempts
- Malware and suspicious activity
- Unusual data transfers
- Policy violations

Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) system aggregates logs from:
- Firewalls and intrusion detection systems
- Authentication systems
- Critical servers and applications

Note: Log retention is currently limited to 90 days due to storage constraints.
Extended retention for compliance requirements is under evaluation.

9.3 Wireless Network Security
Corporate wireless networks use WPA3 encryption and require authentication
via 802.1X using Active Directory credentials.

Guest wireless network is isolated from corporate network and internet usage
is logged.

Personal wireless access points are prohibited on company premises.

==============================================================================
10. INCIDENT RESPONSE AND BUSINESS CONTINUITY
==============================================================================

10.1 Incident Response Plan
Security incidents are categorized as:
- CRITICAL: Data breach, ransomware, major system compromise
- HIGH: Successful phishing attack, malware infection, unauthorized access
- MEDIUM: Failed attack attempts, policy violations, suspicious activity
- LOW: Spam, minor configuration issues

Incident response procedures:
1. Detection and initial assessment (immediate)
2. Containment to prevent further damage (within 2 hours)
3. Investigation to determine scope and impact (within 24 hours)
4. Eradication of threat and restoration of systems (varies by severity)
5. Post-incident review and lessons learned (within 2 weeks)

Critical incidents are reported to executive leadership within 4 hours of
discovery. Data breaches affecting customer information are reported to
affected parties and regulators as required by applicable laws.

10.2 Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery
Business continuity plans are maintained for critical business functions.
Plans are tested annually through tabletop exercises.

Disaster recovery plans exist for critical IT systems. Recovery procedures
are documented and tested quarterly.

Alternate processing facilities have been identified but formal agreements
are not yet in place for all critical systems.

10.3 Vendor and Third-Party Management
Third-party service providers with access to Acme data or systems must:
- Undergo security assessment before contract execution
- Provide evidence of security controls (e.g., SOC 2 reports)
- Agree to contractual security requirements
- Submit to periodic security audits

Note: Legacy vendor contracts do not always include comprehensive cybersecurity
provisions. These are being updated as contracts come up for renewal.

==============================================================================
11. COMPLIANCE AND AUDIT
==============================================================================

11.1 Regulatory Compliance
Acme complies with applicable regulations including:
- Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)
- State data breach notification laws
- Industry-specific regulations as applicable

Compliance assessments are conducted:
- PCI DSS: Annual assessment by Qualified Security Assessor
- Internal security audit: Annual
- External security audit: Every 2 years

11.2 Policy Exceptions
Exceptions to this security policy require:
- Written business justification
- Risk assessment documenting compensating controls
- Approval by CISO
- Time-limited approval (maximum 1 year)
- Annual review for continued necessity

All approved exceptions are tracked and reported quarterly to the
Security Committee.

11.3 Enforcement and Consequences
Violations of this policy may result in:
- Mandatory retraining
- Disciplinary action up to and including termination
- Legal action if violations result in harm to the company
- Reporting to law enforcement for criminal violations

All security incidents are investigated. Intentional policy violations are
treated more severely than inadvertent mistakes.

APPENDIX A: DEFINITIONS
==============================================================================

Authentication: Process of verifying the identity of a user or system
Availability: Ensuring information and systems are accessible when needed
Confidentiality: Protecting information from unauthorized disclosure
Critical Asset: System or data whose loss would severely impact business
Encryption: Process of encoding data to prevent unauthorized access
Incident: Event that compromises security, availability, or integrity
Integrity: Ensuring information is accurate and has not been tampered with
Least Privilege: Providing minimum access needed to perform job duties
Multi-Factor Authentication: Using two or more authentication methods
Vulnerability: Weakness that could be exploited by a threat
